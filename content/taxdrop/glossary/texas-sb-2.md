**Texas Senate Bill 2 (SB 2)**, also known as the **Texas Property Tax Reform and Transparency Act of 2019**, is landmark legislation that fundamentally changed how property taxes are calculated and approved in Texas. The bill was signed into law in June 2019 and represents one of the most significant property tax reforms in Texas history.

## Key Provisions

### Revenue Cap (Rollback Rate) Reduction

The most significant change was lowering the **voter-approval tax rate** (formerly called the "rollback rate"):

| Taxing Entity | Previous Cap | New Cap (SB 2) |
|:--------------|:-------------|:---------------|
| Cities (population >30,000) | 8% | 3.5% |
| Counties | 8% | 3.5% |
| Special districts | 8% | 3.5% |
| Small cities (<30,000) | 8% | 3.5%* |
| School districts | 8% | Separate formula |

*Small cities may petition to use 8% in certain circumstances.

### What the Revenue Cap Means

If a taxing unit wants to increase property tax revenue by more than 3.5% compared to the previous year, voters must approve the increase in an election. This is calculated on **total revenue**, not the tax rate itself.

**Example:**
- Last year's property tax revenue: $10,000,000
- Maximum revenue without election: $10,350,000 (3.5% increase)
- If assessed values increase revenue to $10,500,000 at the same rate, an election is required

### Automatic Elections

Before SB 2, citizens had to petition for a rollback election. Now, elections are **automatic** if a taxing unit exceeds the voter-approval rate.

## Transparency Requirements

SB 2 introduced substantial new transparency measures:

### Tax Rate Notices

Taxing units must provide detailed notices including:
- The **no-new-revenue tax rate** (rate that would produce same revenue as prior year)
- The **voter-approval tax rate** (maximum rate without election)
- The **proposed tax rate**
- Comparison of tax bills on an average home

### Online Database

The Texas Comptroller maintains a publicly accessible database showing:
- Tax rates for all taxing units
- Historical rate comparisons
- Property tax calculator tools

### Hearing Requirements

Public hearings must be held before adopting tax rates, with:
- Minimum public notice periods
- Specific information presented at hearings
- Opportunities for public comment

## Impact on Property Taxes

### For Homeowners

**Potential Benefits:**
- Slower growth in overall property tax bills
- Greater ability to vote on significant tax increases
- Improved access to tax rate information

**Limitations:**
- Does not cap individual property tax bills
- Appraisal increases can still raise your taxes significantly
- Does not affect school district tax rates (separate rules)

### For Local Governments

- Must carefully manage budgets within 3.5% revenue growth
- May need to seek voter approval more frequently
- Increased administrative burden for compliance

## Calculating Your Tax Impact

Property taxes in Texas are calculated as:
```
Property Tax = (Appraised Value - Exemptions) × Tax Rate
```

SB 2 affects the **tax rate** component but not:
- How your property is appraised
- What exemptions you qualify for
- The appraisal review board process

### Example Scenario

| Factor | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|:-------|:-------|:-------|
| Appraised value | $300,000 | $330,000 |
| Homestead exemption | $40,000 | $40,000 |
| Taxable value | $260,000 | $290,000 |
| Tax rate | 2.0% | 1.93%* |
| Property tax bill | $5,200 | $5,597 |

*Rate decreased due to revenue cap, but your bill still increased due to appraisal increase.

## Relationship to Other Texas Tax Laws

### Homestead Exemption

The [Texas Homestead Exemption](/taxdrop/glossary/texas-homestead-exemption/) reduces your taxable value, working alongside SB 2's rate limitations:
- SB 2 limits how fast **rates** can increase revenue
- Homestead exemption reduces your **taxable value**
- Both work together to moderate tax bill growth

### Subsequent Legislation

SB 2 laid the groundwork for additional property tax reforms:

**SB 1 (2019)**: Provided additional school finance reforms
**HB 3**: Reduced school district maintenance and operations tax rates
**SB 2 (2023)**: Further property tax relief including increased homestead exemptions

## Voter-Approval Elections

### When Elections Occur

Elections are held in November if a taxing unit adopts a rate exceeding the voter-approval rate by the required deadline.

### Ballot Language

The ballot must clearly state:
- The taxing unit seeking approval
- The proposed tax rate
- The maximum rate without voter approval
- The percentage increase being requested

### Election Results

If voters **approve**: The higher rate takes effect
If voters **reject**: The taxing unit must adopt the voter-approval rate or lower

## Exemptions from the 3.5% Cap

Certain revenue increases don't count toward the cap:

- Revenue from **new property** added to the tax roll
- Revenue to pay **debt service** (voter-approved bonds)
- Revenue for **disaster recovery** (with state approval)
- Revenue to cover **unfunded mandates** from the state

## Filing a Protest

If you believe your taxing unit isn't complying with SB 2 requirements:

1. Review tax rate notices for required information
2. Attend public hearings and voice concerns
3. Contact the Texas Comptroller's Property Tax Assistance Division
4. Consult with a property tax professional
