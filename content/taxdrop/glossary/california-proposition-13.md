**California Proposition 13**, officially titled the "People's Initiative to Limit Property Taxation," is a landmark constitutional amendment passed by California voters in June 1978. It fundamentally changed how property taxes are calculated in California and remains one of the most significant property tax laws in the United States.

## Key Provisions

### Property Tax Rate Cap
Proposition 13 limits the **maximum property tax rate to 1%** of a property's assessed value. This 1% rate applies to the property's "full cash value," which is typically the purchase price at the time of acquisition.

### Assessment Increase Limitation
Annual increases in assessed value are capped at **no more than 2% per year**, regardless of actual market appreciation. This means even if your home's market value increases by 10% in a year, your assessed value (and therefore your tax bill) can only increase by 2%.

### Reassessment Triggers
A property is reassessed to current market value only when:

- **Change of ownership** occurs (sale, inheritance outside of exemptions, etc.)
- **New construction** is completed on the property
- The property is **damaged or destroyed** and subsequently restored

## Impact on Property Taxes

| Scenario | Without Prop 13 | With Prop 13 |
|:---------|:----------------|:-------------|
| Tax rate | Variable by jurisdiction | Maximum 1% |
| Annual assessment increase | Market-based | Maximum 2% |
| Long-term homeowner benefit | None | Significant savings |

## Example Calculation

If you purchased a home in 2010 for $500,000:

- **Initial assessed value**: $500,000
- **Initial annual tax**: ~$5,000 (1%)
- **2024 assessed value** (with 2% annual increases): ~$660,000
- **2024 annual tax**: ~$6,600

Without Proposition 13, if the market value increased to $1,200,000, your taxes could be $12,000 or more annually.

## Exceptions and Special Rules

### Parent-Child Exclusion (Modified by Prop 19)
Previously, parents could transfer property to children without reassessment. [Proposition 19](/taxdrop/glossary/california-proposition-19/) significantly modified these rules in 2021.

### Base Year Value Transfers
Certain homeowners (seniors, disabled persons, disaster victims) can transfer their Prop 13 base year value to a replacement home under specific conditions.

### Supplemental Assessments
When a property is reassessed due to sale or new construction, the county issues a **supplemental tax bill** to account for the difference between the old and new assessed values for the remainder of the tax year.

## Historical Context

Prior to Proposition 13, California property taxes were based on market value with no cap on rates or assessment increases. During the rapid property value appreciation of the 1970s, many homeowners—particularly seniors on fixed incomes—faced dramatic tax increases that threatened their ability to remain in their homes.

The passage of Prop 13 was led by Howard Jarvis and Paul Gann and sparked a nationwide "tax revolt" that influenced property tax policy in many other states.

## Implications for Homeowners

### Benefits
- Predictable property tax bills with limited annual increases
- Long-term homeowners enjoy substantial tax savings
- Protection against being "taxed out" of your home during market booms

### Considerations
- New buyers pay taxes based on current (higher) market values
- Can create disparities between neighbors with identical homes
- Some argue it reduces incentive to sell and move, limiting housing supply
