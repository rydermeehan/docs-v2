**California Senate Bill 79 (SB 79)** is legislation that expanded and modified the state's **Property Tax Postponement (PTP) Program**, which allows eligible seniors, blind, and disabled homeowners to defer payment of property taxes on their primary residence. The program provides critical relief for homeowners on fixed incomes who might otherwise struggle to pay annual property tax bills.

## Property Tax Postponement Program Overview

The PTP Program allows qualified homeowners to postpone payment of current-year property taxes on their principal residence. The postponed taxes become a lien on the property, similar to a reverse mortgage, with payment deferred until:

- The homeowner sells the property
- The homeowner moves out of the property
- The homeowner passes away
- The property is transferred to a new owner

## Eligibility Requirements

### Basic Requirements

| Requirement | Details |
|:------------|:--------|
| Age/Status | 62 years or older, OR blind, OR disabled |
| Property type | Principal residence (owner-occupied) |
| Equity | At least 40% equity in the property |
| Household income | Annual income at or below program limit |
| Tax status | Property taxes must be current or being brought current |

### Income Limits

The household income limit is adjusted annually by the State Controller's Office. The limit is based on total household income from all sources, including:

- Social Security benefits
- Pension income
- Investment income
- Wages (if any)
- Rental income from the property (if applicable)

### Equity Calculation

Equity is calculated as:
```
Equity = (Market Value - Existing Liens) / Market Value
```

**Example:**
- Home market value: $500,000
- Existing mortgage: $150,000
- Equity calculation: ($500,000 - $150,000) / $500,000 = 70%
- Result: Eligible (exceeds 40% minimum)

## How the Program Works

### Application Process

1. **Obtain application** from the State Controller's Office
2. **Submit application** with required documentation
3. **Property appraisal** conducted if necessary
4. **Approval notification** sent to homeowner and county
5. **Tax payment** made by state to county on homeowner's behalf

### Interest Accrual

Postponed taxes accrue interest at a rate set by the State Controller, typically tied to state borrowing rates. Interest compounds annually and is added to the lien amount.

**Example calculation:**
- Year 1 postponed taxes: $5,000
- Interest rate: 5%
- Year 1 ending balance: $5,250
- Year 2 postponed taxes: $5,100
- Year 2 ending balance: ($5,250 + $5,100) × 1.05 = $10,867.50

### Lien Recording

A lien is recorded against the property securing:
- All postponed property taxes
- Accumulated interest
- Administrative fees

This lien must be satisfied before the property can be sold or transferred with clear title.

## Benefits of the Program

### For Fixed-Income Homeowners
- **Cash flow relief**: Frees up monthly/annual budget for other expenses
- **Stay in your home**: Avoid forced sale due to inability to pay taxes
- **No monthly payments**: Unlike reverse mortgages, no payments required while living in home

### Compared to Other Options

| Option | Monthly Payment | Interest Rate | Equity Access | Requirements |
|:-------|:----------------|:--------------|:--------------|:-------------|
| PTP Program | None | State rate | No | Age/disability, income limit |
| Reverse Mortgage | None | Market rate | Yes | Age 62+, no income limit |
| Home Equity Loan | Yes | Market rate | Yes | Credit/income qualification |

## Program Limitations

### Not Covered
- Supplemental tax bills
- Property taxes on second homes
- Delinquent taxes from prior years (though program can help catch up)
- Special assessments (in some cases)

### Maximum Deferral
The cumulative amount that can be postponed may be limited based on:
- Available equity in the property
- Total lien amount relative to property value

## Relationship to Other California Tax Laws

SB 79 and the PTP Program work alongside other California property tax protections:

### With Proposition 13
The [Proposition 13](/taxdrop/glossary/california-proposition-13/) assessment limitations keep tax bills lower, but even limited amounts can burden fixed-income seniors. PTP provides additional relief by deferring even these reduced amounts.

### With Proposition 19
Seniors who use PTP and later sell to relocate can potentially combine benefits by:
1. Satisfying the PTP lien from sale proceeds
2. Transferring their [Proposition 19](/taxdrop/glossary/california-proposition-19/) tax base to a new home
3. Applying for PTP at the new residence

## Application Information

### Where to Apply
Applications are available from:
- State Controller's Office website
- County tax collector offices
- Senior service organizations

### Required Documentation
- Proof of age, blindness, or disability
- Property deed or title documentation
- Current mortgage statement(s)
- Income verification (tax returns, Social Security statements)
- Property tax bill

### Processing Time
Applications are typically processed within 60-90 days. It's recommended to apply well before tax payment deadlines.
