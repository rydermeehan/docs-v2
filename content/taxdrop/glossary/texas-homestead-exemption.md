The **Texas Homestead Exemption** is a property tax benefit that reduces the taxable value of a homeowner's primary residence. This exemption is one of the most valuable tax benefits available to Texas homeowners and can result in significant annual savings on property tax bills.

## Types of Homestead Exemptions

Texas offers several homestead exemptions, and eligible homeowners can combine multiple exemptions:

### General Residence Homestead Exemption

| Taxing Entity | Exemption Amount |
|:--------------|:-----------------|
| School districts | $100,000 (as of 2023) |
| Cities | Up to 20% of value (minimum $5,000) |
| Counties | Up to 20% of value (minimum $5,000) |
| Special districts | Up to 20% of value (minimum $5,000) |

**Note:** The school district exemption was increased from $40,000 to $100,000 by SB 2 (2023).

### Over-65 Exemption

Homeowners age 65 or older qualify for additional benefits:

| Benefit | Details |
|:--------|:--------|
| Additional school exemption | $10,000 |
| Tax ceiling | School taxes frozen at amount in year you turn 65 |
| Optional exemptions | Cities/counties may offer additional amounts |

### Disabled Person Exemption

Disabled homeowners receive similar benefits to the over-65 exemption:
- Additional $10,000 exemption from school district taxes
- School district tax ceiling (frozen taxes)
- Optional local exemptions

**Note:** You cannot receive both over-65 and disabled exemptions; if eligible for both, you receive the more beneficial one.

### Disabled Veteran Exemption

Veterans with service-connected disabilities qualify for exemptions based on disability rating:

| Disability Rating | Exemption Amount |
|:------------------|:-----------------|
| 10-29% | $5,000 |
| 30-49% | $7,500 |
| 50-69% | $10,000 |
| 70-99% | $12,000 |
| 100% | Full exemption (no property tax) |

### Surviving Spouse Exemptions

Surviving spouses of:
- **Over-65 homeowners**: May continue to receive exemption and tax ceiling
- **Disabled veterans (100%)**: May continue full exemption if not remarried
- **First responders killed in line of duty**: Full exemption

## Eligibility Requirements

### Basic Requirements

To qualify for the general homestead exemption:

| Requirement | Details |
|:------------|:--------|
| Ownership | You must own the property |
| Residency | Must be your principal residence |
| Residency date | Must reside there as of January 1 |
| One homestead | Can only claim one homestead in Texas |
| U.S. residency | Must be U.S. citizen or permanent resident |

### What Qualifies as a Homestead

- Single-family home
- Condominium
- Manufactured home (if you own the land)
- Up to 20 acres of land used as your yard

### What Doesn't Qualify

- Rental properties
- Vacation homes
- Properties held solely for investment
- Business properties

## How to Apply

### Application Process

1. **Obtain the form**: Download Form 50-114 from your county appraisal district or the Texas Comptroller's website
2. **Complete the application**: Provide property information and ownership documentation
3. **Submit documentation**: Include copy of driver's license or state ID showing property address
4. **File with appraisal district**: Submit to your county appraisal district

### Deadlines

| Situation | Deadline |
|:----------|:---------|
| Regular application | April 30 |
| Late application | Up to 2 years after deadline |
| Age 65 application | Any time during the year you turn 65 |

### One-Time Application

Once approved, the homestead exemption remains in effect until:
- You sell the property
- You no longer use it as your principal residence
- Your eligibility changes

You do **not** need to reapply annually.

## Tax Ceiling (Over-65/Disabled)

The tax ceiling is one of the most valuable benefits for seniors and disabled homeowners:

### How It Works

Your school district taxes are "frozen" at the amount you paid in the year you:
- Turned 65, or
- Became disabled

**Example:**
- Year you turn 65: School taxes = $2,500
- 5 years later: School taxes still = $2,500 (even if values increased)

### Ceiling Adjustments

The ceiling may increase only if:
- You make improvements that increase the home's value
- The school district tax rate increases (rare due to state compression)

### Portability

If you sell and buy a new homestead in Texas, you can transfer a **percentage** of your tax ceiling:

```
New Ceiling = (Old Ceiling / Old Value) × New Value
```

**Example:**
- Old home value: $200,000
- Old tax ceiling: $2,000 (1% effective rate)
- New home value: $300,000
- New tax ceiling: $3,000

## Calculating Your Savings

### Example Calculation

| Factor | Without Exemption | With Exemption |
|:-------|:------------------|:---------------|
| Appraised value | $400,000 | $400,000 |
| School exemption | $0 | $100,000 |
| Taxable value (school) | $400,000 | $300,000 |
| School tax rate | 1.0% | 1.0% |
| School taxes | $4,000 | $3,000 |
| **Annual savings** | — | **$1,000** |

This example only shows school district savings. Additional savings apply to city, county, and special district taxes.

### Combined Exemption Example (Over-65)

| Factor | Amount |
|:-------|:-------|
| Appraised value | $400,000 |
| General school exemption | -$100,000 |
| Over-65 school exemption | -$10,000 |
| Taxable value (school) | $290,000 |
| City exemption (20%) | -$80,000 |
| Taxable value (city) | $320,000 |

## Appraisal Cap (10% Limitation)

In addition to exemptions, Texas law limits how much your homestead's appraised value can increase:

- Maximum annual increase: **10%** plus value of improvements
- Applies only to homestead properties
- Helps prevent dramatic tax increases during rapid appreciation

**Example:**
- Year 1 appraised value: $300,000
- Year 2 market value: $400,000
- Year 2 capped value: $330,000 (10% max increase)

## Relationship to Other Texas Tax Laws

### SB 2 Property Tax Reform

[Texas SB 2](/taxdrop/glossary/texas-sb-2/) limits how fast taxing units can increase total revenue, while the homestead exemption reduces your individual taxable value. Both work together to moderate property tax bills.

### Recent Legislative Changes

The 2023 Texas Legislature significantly enhanced homestead benefits:
- Increased school exemption from $40,000 to $100,000
- Provided additional property tax relief through compression

## Common Questions

### Can I claim homestead in multiple states?
No. You can only have one homestead, and it must be your principal residence.

### What if I rent out part of my home?
You may still qualify if you occupy the home as your principal residence. Consult your appraisal district for specific situations.

### Does the exemption apply to new construction?
Yes, but you must apply after the home is complete and you've established residency.

### What if I miss the deadline?
You can file a late application for up to two years after the deadline, but you'll only receive the exemption going forward (no refunds for missed years).
