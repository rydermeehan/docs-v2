**California Proposition 19**, officially titled "The Home Protection for Seniors, Severely Disabled, Families, and Victims of Wildfire or Natural Disasters Act," was approved by California voters in November 2020. It made significant changes to property tax rules affecting inherited properties and expanded benefits for certain homeowners looking to relocate.

## Effective Dates

- **Base year value transfers** (for seniors, disabled, disaster victims): April 1, 2021
- **Parent-child and grandparent-grandchild exclusion changes**: February 16, 2021

## Key Changes

### Expanded Base Year Value Transfers

Proposition 19 allows eligible homeowners to transfer their [Proposition 13](/taxdrop/glossary/california-proposition-13/) tax base to a replacement home:

| Feature | Before Prop 19 | After Prop 19 |
|:--------|:---------------|:--------------|
| Number of transfers | Once (or twice for disasters) | Up to three times |
| Location | Same county or participating county | Anywhere in California |
| Replacement home value | Equal or lesser value | Any value (with adjustment) |
| Age requirement | 55+ or disabled | 55+ or disabled |

### How Base Year Value Transfers Work

If your replacement home costs **more** than your original home:
- You keep your original assessed value
- Plus the difference between the two home prices is added

**Example:**
- Original home assessed value: $200,000
- Original home sale price: $800,000
- New home purchase price: $1,000,000
- New assessed value: $200,000 + ($1,000,000 - $800,000) = $400,000

### Restricted Parent-Child Transfers

This is where Proposition 19 significantly **reduced** benefits that existed under previous law:

#### Before Proposition 19 (Propositions 58 & 193)
- Parents could transfer primary residence to children **without reassessment** (no limit)
- Parents could transfer up to **$1 million** in other property without reassessment
- No requirement for child to live in the property

#### After Proposition 19
- Parent-child exclusion **only applies to primary residences**
- Child **must use the property as their primary residence** within one year
- Exclusion is limited to the **current assessed value plus $1 million**
- **No exclusion** for rental properties, second homes, or commercial property

## Impact on Inherited Properties

### Primary Residence Inheritance

If you inherit your parents' home and plan to live in it:

1. File a homeowner's exemption claim within one year
2. If the property's market value exceeds the assessed value by more than $1 million, the excess is added to the assessed value

**Example:**
- Parent's assessed value: $300,000
- Market value at death: $1,500,000
- Difference: $1,200,000
- Amount over $1 million exclusion: $200,000
- Your new assessed value: $300,000 + $200,000 = $500,000

### Non-Primary Residence Inheritance

Properties that children do not use as their primary residence are **fully reassessed** to current market value, which can result in substantial property tax increases.

## Eligibility Requirements

### For Base Year Value Transfers

| Requirement | Details |
|:------------|:--------|
| Age | 55 years or older, OR |
| Disability | Severely and permanently disabled, OR |
| Disaster victim | Lost home to wildfire or natural disaster |
| Timing | Must file within 2 years of sale/purchase |
| Ownership | Must have owned and occupied original home |

### For Parent-Child Exclusion

| Requirement | Details |
|:------------|:--------|
| Relationship | Parent to child (or grandparent to grandchild if parents deceased) |
| Property type | Primary residence only |
| Use requirement | Child must claim as principal residence within 1 year |
| Filing | Must file claim with county assessor |

## Filing Requirements

To claim Proposition 19 benefits, you must file specific forms with your county assessor:

- **BOE-19-P**: Claim for Transfer of Base Year Value to Replacement Primary Residence
- **BOE-19-G**: Claim for Reassessment Exclusion for Transfer Between Parent and Child

Deadlines are strict, and failure to file timely can result in loss of benefits.

## Strategic Considerations

### For Seniors Considering Downsizing
Proposition 19 removes previous barriers to relocating within California. You can now move anywhere in the state and potentially to a more expensive home while retaining most of your tax base.

### For Estate Planning
The changes to parent-child transfers require families to reconsider estate plans. Options include:
- Children using inherited homes as primary residences
- Trusts structured to maximize remaining exclusions
- Sale of inherited property if tax increase makes holding impractical

### For Investment Property Owners
Rental properties and second homes no longer qualify for the parent-child exclusion. Upon inheritance, these properties are reassessed to market value.
